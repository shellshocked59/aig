## Ten research questions

1. **Is Bounded's premium over Strict modest?** In aggregate there is now a negative difference: Batch 3 used 63 versus 89 requests, a difference of −26 (−29.21%); cumulative totals are 232 versus 238, a difference of −6 (−2.52%). Batch 3 also used 68,891 fewer tokens and 52.38 fewer provider seconds; cumulative differences are −7,087 tokens and −29.99 seconds. However, Bounded uses 1.827 versus 1.308 requests per Luna turn cumulatively, **39.69% more**, and 41.77% more tokens per turn. It had 127 attempted Luna turns versus Strict's 182, with eight versus six forfeits. Strict's 57-player-turn MATCH-022 also affects this batch's totals. Costs remain close in aggregate, but the negative premium is not evidence that repairs or recovered AP are free.

2. **Is Bounded recovering substantial AP?** Yes as a mechanism metric: Batch 3 had 14 initial execution-invalid turns and 14 replans; 13 recovered at least one AP, recovering 26 AP (1.857/replan). Cumulative: 57 replans, 51 positive (89.47%), 103 AP recovered (1.807/replan). Signed extra requests divided by AP recovered is −1.000 for Batch 3 and −0.0583 cumulatively. These are descriptive differences between independent model trajectories, not causal recovery prices. Final truncations are 3 versus Strict's 36 in Batch 3 and 9 versus 82 cumulatively; truncation-derived unused AP is 18 versus 219 cumulatively. Neither arm has won a match, so AP recovery has not established better outcomes.

3. **Is the second-invalid rate acceptable/stable?** Counts by batch are 4/26 (15.38%), 2/17 (11.76%), and 3/14 (21.43%) replans; cumulative 9/57 (15.79%). Batch 3 is higher, with too few observations to establish a trend. No acceptance threshold was frozen. It remains interpretable benchmark data and does not justify tuning. Batch 3 replacement requests required six repairs and had one exhausted repair; cumulative replacement repairs are 19 with four exhausted repairs. Frozen one-replan behavior remained intact.

4. **Are repair forfeits increasing or concentrated?** Counts rose from 2 to 7 to 8 across batches. By control they are Strict 1→3→2, Bounded 1→3→4, Stepwise 0→1→2. Bounded has the largest cumulative count (8/30), followed by Strict (6/30) and Stepwise (3/30). This is elevated relative to Batch 1, not a demonstrated monotonic temporal trend across all controls or an established stochastic cause. Batch 3 repair success was 19/21 Strict, 12/16 Bounded and 12/14 Stepwise. Seven of eight exhausted repairs involved static range/LOS constraints; one exceeded available AP (6 planned versus 5 available) and was recorded as schema validation. Seven occurred with Luna Blue, one Red; this small, post hoc subgroup does not establish side causality. All 437 new provider receipts record returned responses. The failed repairs have request/response evidence, supporting model-output validation failure rather than observed transport outage. These failures materially determine eight Batch 3 losses and 17 cumulative losses; the outcomes absent those forfeits are unknown. Full initial/repair categories, turns, AP, structured actions, actors/targets and receipt evidence appear in [repair forensics](repair-forensics.md).

5. **Does Stepwise's long request tail persist?** Yes. Another 140-request match occurred; capped games consume 560/997 (56.17%) of cumulative Stepwise requests. Its cumulative median match length is only 11 player turns, while cap matches last 92–94. Batch 3's capped game alone consumed 140/285 (49.12%) requests. The request share has declined from 59.0% after Batch 2, but a third consecutive batch with a cap strengthens persistence of the qualitative tail signal; it does not show an increasing cap rate.

6. **How often is Stepwise capped?** Batch 3: 1/10; cumulative: 4/30 (13.33%), versus 3/20 (15%) previously. MATCH-001, 009, 015 and 021 all stopped under REQUEST_LIMIT with no winner. MATCH-021 reached 94 player turns, 46 completed rounds and 47 Luna turns, executing 233 AP/139 actions with zero repairs and one explicit EndTurn. All four retained red-core HP 12 and blue-core HP 30. Full unit states, every cap's counts, and state hashes are in [cap details](cap-details.json). All four are Red; natural uncapped outcomes remain censored and unknown. The frozen cap stays 140.

7. **Are meaningful outcome differences emerging?** There is no Bounded–Strict win separation: both are 0/30. Stepwise has 2/30 wins, 24 losses and four no-results; it added no win in Batch 3. Wilson 95% intervals are 0–11.35% for Strict/Bounded and 1.85–21.32% for Stepwise. Matched triplets comprise 24 all-loss, two Stepwise-only-win, and four Stepwise-cap/others-loss cases; there are no Strict-only or Bounded-only wins. This is a limited descriptive Stepwise win signal with substantial uncertainty and cost, not a final control ranking. No final-study p-values are asserted at n=30/control.

8. **Does Heuristic V2 still overwhelm Luna?** Yes descriptively: it wins 30/30 versus Strict, 30/30 versus Bounded and 24/30 versus Stepwise (two losses, four no-results). These are separate matchup baselines. Strict and Bounded have dealt zero Core damage cumulatively; Stepwise has dealt 30. The frozen opponent remains unchanged. Poor Luna play is evidence, not a reason to alter the study.

9. **Are EndTurn semantics consistent?** Yes. Batch 3 added 52 explicit stops, for 178 cumulatively (65 Strict, 51 Bounded, 62 Stepwise), with **zero subsequent provider requests within those turns**. Immediate stops number 1/0/15 cumulatively. Legal damaging options remained at 24/11/6 stops; these are mechanical opportunity observations, not proof that attacking was tactically preferable. Intentional unused AP is reported separately from clean completion, truncation, provider failure, request-budget denial and terminal AP. The frozen generic failure bucket includes eight cumulative Stepwise AP denied by request limits; the reports explicitly separate this from 12 actual provider-failure AP.

10. **Is there evidence the frozen contract is defective?** No defect was found in source checks, exact replay, schedule/accounting/seals or the structured failure evidence. The broad recorded invalid_reference category can represent a first action outside the current legal catalog even when IDs exist; the separate audit identifies the geometry constraints without changing official categories. The AP-invalid repaired plan violates the current budget. These observations support retaining ordinary model failures as data. Exact replay alone cannot prove the intended design flawless, but there is no observed hard-stop condition or basis for changing the contract.

## Cost and outcome tradeoff

Bounded remains substantially cheaper than Stepwise: cumulative requests 232 versus 997, tokens 653,148 versus 2,552,336 and provider seconds 406.58 versus 1,594.97. Per attempted Luna turn, Bounded uses about 40.82% fewer requests, 34.92% fewer tokens and 35.17% less provider time. It has zero caps versus four, but more repair forfeits (eight versus three) and fewer wins (zero versus two). Thus the hoped-for claim that Bounded improves Strict outcomes/reliability while retaining low cost is **not established**. Recovery and low aggregate cost coexist with weak match outcomes and material repair failure.

Batch 3 used 1,170,527 tokens and 12.10 provider minutes; measured process wall elapsed was at most 17.84 minutes, including prefix verification and completion observation but excluding later reporting. Cumulative totals are 3,865,719 tokens, 40.64 provider minutes and 54.07 observed process wall minutes. Provider/backend time overlap and should not be added. Cached input is included in input; reasoning tokens are included in output. Frozen pricing is absent, so no dollar estimate is supplied.
